UNCOMMON VALUE

Dance is the vehicle. The value is what the vehicle can deliver.

Our advantage is not that ballroom dancing does one thing extremely well. It is that the same vehicle can deliver many forms of wellness — and we can change the dose over time.

1. The Vehicle Is Adjustable

Physical

From slightly harder than walking to serious athletic training.

Mental

From learning a few basic patterns to complex timing, technique, memory and musicality.

Social

From one trusted teacher to a full community, events, friendships and belonging.

Emotional / Relational

From fun and stress relief to confidence, identity, connection and shared experiences.

Same vehicle. Different person. Different dose.

2. We Are Not Diagnosing People

We may not truly know what a client needs for months or even years. They may not know either. And what they need can change.

Our job: notice patterns, form a reasonable hypothesis, create value, watch the response, and adjust.

Patterns are clues — not conclusions.

3. Our Staff Framework

PERSON
EXPLAIN THE VEHICLE
LISTEN FOR PATTERNS
DOSE THE VALUE

PERSON: Who is in front of me?

EXPLAIN THE VEHICLE: Help them understand what dance can become for them.

LISTEN FOR PATTERNS: What do they repeatedly say, avoid, value, complain about or light up around?

DOSE THE VALUE: Which Human Need or Wellness Pillar might deserve a little more attention right now?

4. Pattern Examples

“Everything is about my husband and kids.”

Possible clue: over-connected identity.

Possible dose: significance, personal growth, individual goals.

“We do the same thing every weekend.”

Possible clue: routine / predictability.

Possible dose: variety, uncertainty, new shared experiences.

“I’m worried I won’t be good at this.”

Possible clue: uncertainty or fear of failure.

Possible dose: certainty, small wins, encouragement, clear progress.

“What’s next?”

Possible clue: desire for challenge or progress.

Possible dose: growth, variety, stronger goals.

5. Human Needs Are Sliders

We are not choosing one need and ignoring the others. Think of the six Human Needs as sliders on a mixing board.

CertaintyVariety / UncertaintySignificanceLove / ConnectionGrowthContribution

The question is not: “Which one do they need?”

The better question is: “Which one might they need a little more of right now?”

Closing Principle

We don't define the person. We adjust the dose.

Our uncommon value is that ballroom dance can continuously change with the person — across their goals, stage of life, relationships, health, identity and interests.
